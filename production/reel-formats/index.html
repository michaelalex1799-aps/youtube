White Nights Reel Lab

Five reel formats for narrating the book, each with its own palette, type, motion and narrator voice. The first phone is a real render from the production pipeline. Below the formats: which voice to use for each one, and the whole book cut into a swipe-through series.

Reel formats

Silent looping previews (10–12 s) to compare looks. Tap a phone to pause it. Finished reels run 45–75 s with voice-over and music.

The real render is fully narrated (British storyteller voice) with music ducked under the voice. Press "Sound off" on it to turn the sound on.

Voice playbook

What narration styles fit each format. Book-narration channels with big views tend to use one of three voice families: a deep, unhurried storyteller for story reels; an intimate close-mic voice for quote and sleep content; and a fast, conversational voice for "POV" and explainer hooks. Pick one as the channel's signature voice and use the others only where a format needs them.

Hear the narrators

Seven generated voices reading the same hook and opening line, one per playbook style. Generated with the open-source Kokoro model, so they can be used freely. Pick the series voice by ear, then A/B test it.

Test two voices per format

Post the same reel with two narrators (Instagram trial reels or two uploads a week apart). Keep the one with the higher 3-second hold and average watch time.

One signature voice

The series narrator is the channel's identity. Viewers binge parts 1→N because the voice carries them; don't switch it mid-book.

AI voice rules

AI narration can earn if the reel adds original writing (hooks, bridges, commentary). Turn on YouTube's altered/synthetic content label when a voice sounds like a real person.

Second language later

Once a format works, dub the same visuals in Hindi. It reuses every graphic and opens a second audience.

The whole book as a swipe series

Every part opens on a hook in the first two seconds, moves fast (a new shot every 2–6 s), and ends on a cliffhanger that sends viewers to the next part. Scroll inside the phone, or use the part buttons, to see each reel's script, shots and graphics.
