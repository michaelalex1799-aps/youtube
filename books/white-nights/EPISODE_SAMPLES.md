# White Nights: sample plan for all 6 episodes

Source: Fyodor Dostoevsky, *White Nights* (1848), translated by Constance Garnett (1918, public domain).
Narration pace is about 150 words per minute. Times include storytelling breaks.

| # | Episode | Book section | Words | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Man Who Talked to Houses | First Night | 4,500 | ~32 min |
| 2 | "I Am a Dreamer" | Second Night, the Dreamer's story | 5,400 | ~38 min |
| 3 | The Girl Pinned to Her Grandmother | Second Night, Nastenka's story | 3,800 | ~27 min |
| 4 | The Rainy Day | Third Night | 2,600 | ~19 min |
| 5 | "I Love You, Nastenka" | Fourth Night | 3,600 | ~26 min |
| 6 | One Moment of Happiness | Morning | 900 | ~10 min |

Visual code for the whole series:
- **Present:** cold blue night, gold streetlights.
- **Memories:** warm amber blur.
- **Daydreams:** soft glowing, fantasy-like colors.
- **Thoughts:** handwritten text.
- **Progress:** a small moon in the corner fills as the nights go on (Night 1 = thin crescent, Morning = sunrise).

## Episode 1: The Man Who Talked to Houses (First Night)

**Title:** *He Had No Friends for 8 Years, Until One Night… | White Nights Ep. 1*
**Thumbnail:** a lone man on a canal bridge at night, a woman's silhouette at the railing far away. Text: **"8 YEARS ALONE"**

**Hook (original, 0:00–0:30):**
> "Eight years in one of the biggest cities in the world, and not a single friend. Except the houses. He talked to the houses. And one of them talked back. This is the story of four nights that changed a lonely man's life. Written by Dostoevsky, 174 years ago, about someone who might be you."

**Scenes:**
1. Starry night sky, a slow pan down to the empty summer streets of St. Petersburg, under the opening line *"It was a wonderful night, such a night as is only possible when we are young, dear reader."*
2. The city emptying for summer: carriages leaving, windows closing one by one.
3. **The houses scene:** buildings with soft, subtle "faces" in their windows. The little pink house turns yellow while he says *"They are painting me yellow!"*. Gentle humor works for every age.
4. A woman at the canal railing, crying. The music drops to a single piano note.
5. A man follows her. The Dreamer saves her and she takes his arm. A warm color creeps into the blue for the first time.
6. They agree to meet tomorrow. End on the moon: a thin crescent.

**Storytelling breaks (your own words):**
- *~8 min:* "Notice something? We never learn his name. Dostoevsky leaves it blank on purpose. He could be anyone, even you."
- *~20 min:* "In 1848 St. Petersburg emptied every summer. The rich left for country villas. Imagine being the one who stays behind."

**Outro:** "Tomorrow night, she asks for his story. And he has never told it to anyone. Would you have gone back to that bridge?"

**Short from this episode:** *"POV: you're so lonely you make friends with buildings."* Use the "pink house painted yellow" moment with fast captions. 35 seconds.

## Episode 2: "I Am a Dreamer" (Second Night, first part)

**Title:** *Dostoevsky Described Introverts Perfectly in 1848 | White Nights Ep. 2*
**Thumbnail:** a man in a dark room lit only by a glowing daydream above his head. Text: **"ARE YOU A DREAMER?"**

**Hook:**
> "'A dreamer is not a human being, but a creature of an intermediate sort.' If you've ever lived more in your head than in real life, this episode is about you."

**Scenes:**
1. The bridge, a second night. Nastenka is waiting and laughing: *"Well, here you are."*
2. She asks for his "history". He says he has none, and she teases him.
3. **The dreamer monologue (centerpiece):** the visual style changes completely. His dark room fills with glowing fantasy scenes (knights, lovers, travels) that fade the moment he "wakes".
4. A slow return to the bridge; Nastenka is listening, moved.
5. Dark room, faded dreams, cobwebs: *"in vain the dreamer rakes over his old dreams"*.

**Storytelling breaks:**
- "This monologue is one of the most quoted passages in Russian literature. Young readers online say it's the first time a book described them."
- "Dostoevsky was 26 when he wrote this. A year later he was arrested and sentenced to death. He was saved minutes before execution."

**Outro:** "He told her everything. Now it's her turn, and her story changes everything for him."

**Short:** the dreamer definition quote, word-by-word captions over glowing fantasy scenes that fade to a dark room. Text: *"Dostoevsky called you out in 1848."*

## Episode 3: The Girl Pinned to Her Grandmother (Second Night, second part)

**Title:** *Her Grandmother Pinned Her Dress So She Couldn't Leave | White Nights Ep. 3*
**Thumbnail:** two dresses joined by a safety pin; a girl looking at a window. Text: **"PINNED FOR 2 YEARS"**

**Hook:**
> "Her grandmother was blind. And to stop her from running away, she pinned her granddaughter's dress to her own. For two years. Then a stranger moved in upstairs."

**Scenes (amber memory color throughout):**
1. The small room, the blind grandmother knitting, the pin between them.
2. The new lodger: a young man, books, a promised trip to the opera.
3. Nastenka jumps up in embarrassment, *"so that grandmother's chair moved"* (gentle humor).
4. The opera night, the growing feeling.
5. The lodger leaves for Moscow for a year, with a promise. She climbs the stairs to his room with her bundle.
6. Back to the present: blue night. *"A year has passed. He is in Petersburg. And he hasn't come."*

**Storytelling breaks:**
- "Notice how the Dreamer listens. For the first time in eight years, someone is telling *him* a story."
- "Here's the cruel twist: the Dreamer is falling for a girl who is waiting for someone else. And he agrees to help her."

**Outro:** "He offers to deliver her letter to the man she loves. Would you do the same?"

**Short:** *"Her grandma pinned her dress to hers so she couldn't leave."* A storybook-style animation. This one also works for older viewers and families.

## Episode 4: The Rainy Day (Third Night)

**Title:** *When You Love Someone Who's Waiting for Someone Else | White Nights Ep. 4*
**Thumbnail:** rain on a window, a man's reflection, a lit bridge outside. Text: **"SHE'S WAITING FOR HIM"**

**Hook:**
> "'To-day was a gloomy, rainy day without a glimmer of sunlight, like the old age before me.' Night three. The letter has been delivered. She is happy, and he is breaking."

**Scenes:**
1. Rain on glass, gray light. This is the only grey palette in the series.
2. The bridge at night. Nastenka is restless and joyful, waiting.
3. She is kind to him, too kind. She takes his hand.
4. Hours pass. The man doesn't come. Show it with a clock and the streetlights flickering out.
5. She cries, and he comforts her while hiding his own feelings.

**Storytelling breaks:**
- "This is the shortest night, and the hardest one. Dostoevsky makes you feel the waiting."
- "Psychologists have a name for this: loving someone and staying silent so they stay close. How many of us have done it?"

**Outro:** "Tomorrow, he can't hold it in any longer."

**Short:** a split screen with her waiting happily and him watching her. Text: *"Loving someone who's waiting for someone else."* Rain sounds.

## Episode 5: "I Love You, Nastenka" (Fourth Night)

**Title:** *He Finally Said It, Then Everything Changed | White Nights Ep. 5*
**Thumbnail:** two figures close on the bridge, a third figure approaching in the dark. Text: **"HE SAID IT"**

**Hook:**
> "'It's impossible, but I love you, Nastenka! There it is! Now everything is told.' Four nights. One confession. And a stranger walking toward them."

**Scenes:**
1. Nastenka, hurt: the man hasn't come. She is crying at the railing.
2. **The confession:** everything else drops away, only the two figures and one gold light. Music is silent for the line itself.
3. Hope. They walk and plan a future, and the colors warm up for the first time in the series.
4. A man passes them in the dark, stops, and says her name.
5. She runs to him, comes back to kiss the Dreamer, then runs again. Freeze frame. The Dreamer alone on the bridge.

**Storytelling breaks:**
- "For a few minutes in this chapter, Dostoevsky lets us believe. That's what makes the next moment hurt."
- (No break after the ending. Let the silence sit for 3–4 seconds.)

**Outro:** "One night is left. Not a night. A morning. And a letter."

**Short:** the confession line read alone over silence, then a single piano note. Text: *"Dostoevsky, 1848. Still the bravest line in literature."*

## Episode 6: One Moment of Happiness (Morning)

**Title:** *The Most Beautiful Last Line in Literature | White Nights Finale*
**Thumbnail:** sunrise through a dusty window, a letter on the table. Text: **"ENOUGH FOR A LIFETIME?"**

**Hook:**
> "The nights are over. It's morning. There's a knock at the door, and a letter."

**Scenes:**
1. Sunrise, but the colors are dull. His small room.
2. **Nastenka's letter**, shown as her handwriting on screen while it's read: *"Forgive me, remember and love your Nastenka."* Use a softer, younger second voice if possible.
3. Matrona, the old servant: *"I have taken all the cobwebs off the ceiling."*
4. The room and the house opposite age before his eyes: peeling walls, cracks. He sees himself *"fifteen years hence… just as solitary."* Use visible time-lapse aging.
5. The turn: no bitterness. *"May your sky be clear… may you be blessed."* Light slowly returns.
6. **Final line, full screen, gold, slow:**
   *"My God, a whole moment of happiness! Is that too little for the whole of a man's life?"*
   Hold it for 5 seconds of silence before the music returns.

**Closing reflection (original, ~2 min):**
> "Most love stories end with two people together. This one ends with a man alone, and he is grateful. Dostoevsky asks us one question: if you could have one perfect moment with someone, knowing it would end, would you take it? Tell me in the comments."

**End screen:** "Next book: *Notes from Underground*, the Dreamer's darker brother." Link the full White Nights sleep edition.

**Short:** the final line over sunrise. This is the most shareable clip of the whole series. Make three versions (English captions, Hindi subtitles, and a version for older viewers with large text).
