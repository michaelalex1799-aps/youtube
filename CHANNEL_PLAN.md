# YouTube channel plan: classic books, narrated so people stay

Goal: **1,000,000 total views** with a channel that narrates books in an engaging way, starting with *White Nights* (Dostoevsky, Constance Garnett translation).

---

## 1. The most important decision: don't be "just an audiobook"

YouTube's monetization rules say that **a video that only reads out text you didn't write may not earn money** (the "reused content" policy). They also bar **"generic or repetitive" mass-produced content**, especially AI-voiced, template-made videos with no original input (the July 2025 "inauthentic content" update). Channels that only read a book aloud with an AI voice over a still image are the ones getting demonetized now.

Plain audiobook channels such as Greatest AudioBooks (about 1M subscribers, about 125M views) grew big years ago. A new copy of that format today competes with LibriVox, Audible, Spotify and thousands of AI clones.

**So the format is: narration + your own storytelling layer.** Each video mixes:
- **The real text**, narrated with feeling (your own voice, or one consistent voice character).
- **Your original writing**: a hook, short "what's really happening here" breaks, character insight, historical context, and a closing reflection.
- **Visuals that add something**: mood scenes of St. Petersburg, on-screen quotes, the character's inner thoughts as text.

That makes each video original work, so it can be monetized, and it's also what keeps viewers watching.

## 2. Why *White Nights* is a strong first book

- It went **viral with Gen Z** on Instagram, BookTok and in India (2023 onward). Its last line, *"My God, a whole moment of happiness! Is that too little for the whole of a man's life?"* is shared constantly (other translations word it as "a moment of bliss"). There's existing demand.
- It's **short** (four "Nights" plus a "Morning"), so you can cover it as a series in a few weeks.
- It's **emotional** (loneliness, unrequited love, the dreamer), which produces comments and shares.
- **Copyright:** Dostoevsky's original is public domain. Constance Garnett's translation (1918) is public domain in the US and most countries. Keep the source as this translation; don't use modern translations, which are still under copyright.

## 3. Channel positioning

**Working concept:** *"Lonely classics, read at night."* Emotional, calm, beautiful literature for people who feel things deeply. It's late-night listening, not a study aid.

Pick one lane and stay there for the first 30 videos. Good follow-up books in the same mood, all public domain:
- Dostoevsky: *Notes from Underground*, *A Gentle Creature*, *The Dream of a Ridiculous Man*, *Poor Folk*
- Chekhov short stories (*The Lady with the Dog*), Tolstoy's *The Death of Ivan Ilyich*
- Kafka (*The Metamorphosis*), Gogol (*The Overcoat*), Oscar Wilde's fairy tales, Edgar Allan Poe

Channel name ideas: *The Dreamer's Library*, *Night Readings*, *Quiet Classics*. Check that the name is free on YouTube and Instagram before deciding.

## 4. Three content formats

| Format | Length | Purpose | Example for *White Nights* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shorts / Reels** | 30–60 s | Discovery and reach (this is where most early views come from) | One emotional quote, narrated over a night-street scene, with the hook "Dostoevsky wrote this about loneliness in 1848…" |
| **Episodes** | 15–30 min | Main watch time, the series | "White Nights, Night 1: The Man Who Talked to Houses", narration plus 2–3 short storytelling breaks |
| **Full "sleep" edition** | 1.5–3 h | Long watch-time video that keeps getting views | The whole book, calm narration, rain and soft music, dark visuals, after the series ends |

Optional fourth format, once the channel grows: **"Explained in 10 minutes"** videos ("Why everyone is crying over White Nights"). These are pure commentary and pull in a curious, non-reading audience.

## 5. What makes a video "engaging"

1. **The first 30 seconds decide everything.** Open with an emotional line or a question, not "Welcome to my channel". For example: *"He had lived eight years in St. Petersburg and had not made a single friend. Then, one night, he heard a woman crying by the canal."*
2. **Voice:** warm, unhurried, with real pauses. Use a decent USB mic (about $50–100), record in a quiet room under a blanket, and remove noise lightly. A human voice is the strongest protection against monetization problems. If you use AI voice, write a lot of original commentary and disclose it.
3. **Sound design:** soft piano or strings, ambient city or rain. Use music from YouTube Audio Library or licensed sources only.
4. **Visuals:** consistent style across the channel (for example dark blue nights, painterly St. Petersburg, one recurring "Dreamer" figure). Change shots every 5–10 seconds. Show key lines as on-screen text.
5. **Storytelling breaks** every 4–6 minutes: one short original insight ("Notice he's never told us his name…"). These keep viewers watching and make the video original work.
6. **Chapters** in every long video, plus a **series playlist** so viewers go straight to the next episode.

## 6. Titles and thumbnails

- Title emotion first, book second: *"The Loneliest Love Story Ever Written | White Nights by Dostoevsky (Part 1)"* rather than *"White Nights Audiobook Chapter 1"*.
- Thumbnail: one face or figure, night colors, **3–4 words max** ("HE WAITED FOUR NIGHTS"). Make 2–3 versions and use YouTube's built-in thumbnail A/B test.
- In the description, include the book, the author, the translator (Garnett), a public-domain note and the chapters.

## 7. Path to 1,000,000 views (realistic timeline)

| Phase | Weeks | Output | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setup** | 0–2 | Channel, branding, mic, editing template, 10 Shorts ready | — |
| **White Nights launch** | 3–8 | 5 episodes (one per Night + Morning), 3–5 Shorts per week, then the full sleep edition | 50K–150K views, mostly from Shorts |
| **Second and third books** | 9–20 | Two more series from the list above; double down on the best-performing Short style | 300K–500K cumulative |
| **Scale** | 21–36 | One series a month, weekly Shorts, a few "Explained" videos | **1M cumulative** |

Monetization (YouTube Partner Program) needs **1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months** (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days). Long sleep editions are the fastest way to the watch hours.

**Honest expectation:** 1M total views in 6–12 months is achievable with consistency (3–5 Shorts plus one long video every week). One viral Short can speed it up a lot, but plan for steady growth, not luck.

## 8. Weekly workflow

1. **Mon:** Script the episode. Mark text sections and write your hook, breaks and outro.
2. **Tue:** Record narration (about 30–40 min of raw audio).
3. **Wed–Thu:** Edit audio, add visuals, music, captions and chapters.
4. **Fri:** Make thumbnail versions; cut 3–5 Shorts from the episode's best lines.
5. **Sat:** Publish the episode; schedule the Shorts through the week.
6. **Sun:** Check analytics (retention graph, click-through rate). Find where viewers drop off and fix that in the next script.

**Tools:** Audacity or Adobe Podcast (audio), DaVinci Resolve or CapCut (editing), Canva (thumbnails), YouTube Studio analytics.

## 9. Numbers to watch

- **Click-through rate:** aim for above 5%. If it's lower, change the thumbnail or title.
- **First 30 seconds retention:** aim for above 70%. If it's lower, rewrite the hook.
- **Average view duration on episodes:** aim for above 40%.
- **Shorts swiped away:** a low rate means the first second works.
- **Comments:** ask one emotional question per video ("Would you have waited?"). Comments help the algorithm.

## 10. Visual engagement across age groups

**One rule:** the *story* is for everyone; the *format* changes by age. Shorts mostly reach under-30s. Long videos and sleep editions are watched a lot by 35+ viewers, often on a TV.

### What each age group responds to

| Age | Where they watch | What hooks them visually | Pacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13–24** | Phone, Shorts, Reels | Bold captions word by word, aesthetic "night city" visuals, relatable text ("POV: you fell for someone in 4 nights") | Cut every 1–3 s |
| **25–40** | Phone and laptop, often with headphones | Cinematic scenes, quote cards, small context pop-ups (map of St. Petersburg, 1848) | Cut every 4–8 s |
| **40–60** | Laptop and TV | Clean painterly scenes, chapter titles, a calm narrator | Cut every 8–15 s |
| **60+** | TV, tablet | Large readable text, high contrast, no flashing, steady images, a warm voice | Slow, gentle movement |
| **Under 13** | — | *White Nights* is a love story for older audiences. Kids' content would need different books and a separate channel (YouTube's "made for kids" rules turn off comments and personalized ads). | — |

### Visual system that works for all of them

1. **One consistent world.** The same color palette (deep blues, gold streetlights), the same recurring "Dreamer" figure and the same title style in every video. Every age recognizes the brand at a glance.
2. **"Living painting" style.** Painterly still scenes with slow camera movement (zoom, pan), soft fog, flickering lamps and moving water. It's calm enough for older viewers and beautiful enough for younger ones.
3. **Show emotion, not decoration.**
   - Character's thoughts: handwritten text appearing on screen.
   - Memories or dreams: soft blur, warm tint.
   - The present: cold blue night.
   - The time of night: a small clock or moon in the corner.

   Viewers of all ages follow the story more easily this way.
4. **Text rules:** captions always on, 2 lines max, large font, high contrast. Highlight key quotes in gold so viewers screenshot them, which brings free shares.
5. **Light structure aids:** "Night 1 of 4" progress bars, chapter cards, and a 10-second "previously…" recap at the start of each episode. These help older viewers and anyone who drops in partway.
6. **A pattern interrupt every 4–6 minutes** in long videos: a change of scene, a pop-up of a historical fact, or a cut to a storytelling break. This stops attention from fading.
7. **Sleep editions are different:** few cuts, dark screens, no bright flashes or sudden sounds.

### The same moment, three ways

The last line, *"My God, a whole moment of happiness!…"*:
- **Short:** word-by-word bold captions, fast zoom into the canal at night, a 2-second dark beat at the end, text overlay "Dostoevsky, 1848".
- **Episode:** slow pan across the empty bridge, the quote appears in gold handwriting, a soft piano swell.
- **Sleep edition:** a still image of the canal with gently moving water; the quote fades in and stays on screen.

### Accessibility checklist

- [ ] Accurate captions (fix YouTube's auto-captions) and translated subtitles: Hindi, Spanish, Portuguese
- [ ] Text at least 5% of screen height and readable on a TV across the room
- [ ] No rapid flashing (safe for people with epilepsy)
- [ ] Music at least 20 dB below the voice
- [ ] Thumbnails tested at phone size and at TV size
